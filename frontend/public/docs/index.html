Welcome to the TraceIQ guide

Every page of TraceIQ, explained in plain words: what it is for, what each part of the screen means, and how to use it. You don't need to be a tester or a developer to follow it.

What TraceIQ does, in one minute

Before a website or app reaches its users, someone has to check that it still works: that people can sign in, that the checkout still takes payments, that a page didn't break after the latest change. Doing that by hand every day is slow and easy to forget.

TraceIQ does those checks automatically. You describe a check once (a test: "open the home page, click Sign in, type the email, expect to see the dashboard"), and TraceIQ runs it in a real browser whenever you want: on a timer, when a developer changes code, or with one click. Each time, it records what happened, with screenshots, video and logs, and tells you clearly what passed and what failed. When something fails, it helps you find out why.

1 · Write a test

Describe the steps a person would take, and what should happen.

2 · Run it

TraceIQ runs it in a real browser, as often as you need.

3 · Read the result

Passed or failed, with the evidence to show why.

A few words you will see everywhere

Test (or test case)
One check, written as a list of steps. For example "a visitor can add an item to the basket".
Suite
A folder of related tests, such as "Checkout" or "Sign-in". Suites can hold smaller suites inside them.
Run
One execution of a suite or a single test. Every run gets its own page with the results.
Project
Everything that belongs to one product or website: its suites, runs, settings and people. You pick the active project at the top of the app's sidebar.
Workspace
A team's space. It holds one or more projects and the people who can use them.
Pass rate
Out of the runs that finished with a verdict, the share that passed. 9 passed out of 10 = 90%.

How this guide is organised

The guide follows the app's own sidebar, top to bottom. Each group below is a group in the sidebar, and each page in the guide matches one item in it, so you can always find the help for the screen you are looking at.

How each page is laid out

Every page in this guide follows the same pattern, so you always know where to look:

  1. In short: the page explained in one or two sentences.
  2. What it is for: the question it answers, and who usually uses it.
  3. A tour of the screen: a screenshot with numbered parts, then an explanation of each number. Click any screenshot to see it larger.
  4. A short video: the page in use, with captions.
  5. How to…: step-by-step answers to common tasks.
  6. Good to know: the details that are easy to miss.
✓
Where the pictures come from The screenshots and videos are taken from a demo workspace filled with example data, so names like "Web smoke (demo)" are samples. Your own screens will show your projects.
